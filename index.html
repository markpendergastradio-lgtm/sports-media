Project 2

Radio sports news bulletin

UNIT 5 · YOUTHS CHOICE · RCS ZETTA

You run the sports desk. Find the stories, cut the clips, voice a 3-minute bulletin, load it into Zetta and play it out live. Then a late story breaks and you shake the running order up on the fly.

  1. 1 · Source the stories. Pick the five biggest sports stories of the day from IRN, Sky Sports News wire copy and club and league sources. Check every fact in two places and rank the stories: new today, matters to your listeners, has a clip that brings it to life. Biggest first, lighter or local last.
  2. 2 · Edit the clips. Cut at least three clips of 10–20 seconds in Adobe Audition. Top and tail them, remove clicks and dead air, balance them to −16 LUFS, and log each clip's in-words, out-words and duration.
  3. 3 · Write and voice. Every word is yours: short sentences, numbers written the way you say them ("two-nil"), and every clip introduced and back-announced. Record your links on the studio mic.
  4. 4 · Load into Zetta. Ingest your voice links, clips and the sports news bed into RCS Zetta. Label every item in the house format (for example SurnameDDMMYY Story 1), set your segue and ducking points, and build the bulletin on the log.
  5. 5 · Shake it up and go live. Play your bulletin out from Zetta to time. Then your teacher drops in a late story. Re-rank, rewrite and re-cut, rebuild the log, and play out the new version with no dead air.

Hand in

  • Running order with scripts, clip in/out words and durations
  • Both bulletins as MP3 (first and shaken-up versions)
  • Zetta log screenshot showing every item labelled and timed
  • Edit timeline screenshots in your weekly log

Pass

Five stories, three introduced clips, about 3 minutes, clear voice, loaded into Zetta and played out.

Merit and Distinction

Merit: sensible ranking, even levels, clean segues, no gaps. Distinction: sounds like a professional bulletin, timed to 2:50–3:10, with a confident live rework when the late story breaks.

Brief

The Final Whistle: football radio report

ADOBE AUDITION

A professional-standard football match report built in Adobe Audition, covering research, scripting, voice-over, editing and mixing for a target audience.

  1. Assemble a sports report from supplied voice-over, manager quotes and sound effects. Submit as MP3 with timeline screenshots.
  2. Deconstruct and research: analyse professional radio reports for structure, pacing, tone and sound (300 words), then gather team news, stats and story angles.
  3. Script and plan: a 2-minute script (300–350 words) with audio cues and manager soundbites, plus a production plan.
  4. Produce and mix: record an authoritative voice-over, then balance voice, manager clips and crowd atmos in a multitrack session. Export a high-quality MP3.
  5. Publish and evaluate: post the package and script on WordPress with a 300-word critical evaluation of your choices, challenges and audience impact.

Specialist Study: The Platform Revolution

A written essay taking a position on how sport's media landscape is changing, and a 3–5 minute sports podcast episode that argues that position hard. Explore content distribution ecosystems, audience fragmentation and the evolution of sports broadcasting platforms.

Deliver

What you hand in

  • A Unit 12 tab on your WordPress portfolio
  • A referenced, critical essay with a clear argument
  • A 3–5 minute sports podcast episode that takes a robust critical position on your essay's argument
  • A fully realised script with vocal delivery notes
  • Production schedules
  • A clean multitrack master with editorial sound design and balanced music beds, embedded on your WordPress blog
  • Weekly log entries for every stage
Format

Choose your podcast format

Investigative featureRadio debateAudio documentaryDeep-dive sports media explainer

Pick the format that best serves your argument and your audience, and explain that choice in your weekly log.

Questions

What your essay can explore

  • How have rights, distribution and audiences moved from traditional TV, radio and print to streaming, social, YouTube, podcasts and club channels?
  • Is the audience fragmenting, and who holds it now?
  • How do athletes and clubs reach fans directly, and what does that mean for broadcasters and journalists?
  • What are the strengths, limits and business models of old and new platforms?
Build it

A structure that works

ESSAY
  • Introduction: the question, your definitions, your argument.
  • Case studies: two or three organisations compared with evidence.
  • Analysis: figures, quotes and sources, weighing both sides.
  • Conclusion: answer the question and set up your podcast's stance.
  • References: academic, industry and news sources, consistently referenced.

How to write a weekly log

Eight sections, one brilliant log

Your weekly log is evidence of your thinking, not a diary of what you did. Work through the eight sections for your year and answer every prompt in your own words.

Rule 1

Show, don't tell

Screenshots, screen recordings, photos and diagrams beat description every time.

Rule 2

Link your research

Reviews, manuals, academic papers, examples: hyperlink them at the point you mention them.

Rule 3

Keep it building

Write a section well once, then copy, paste and add to it as the project progresses.

    Submit

    Before you submit

    • Every section has at least one screenshot, photo or diagram.
    • Every source, example and how-to guide is hyperlinked.
    • You've explained why, not just what.
    • Your three problems are matched to three solutions.
    • Three things that went well and three to improve are written down.
    • You've referred back to an earlier log to show progression.
    • Spelling, grammar and file names are checked. This is a professional document.
    Check

    Log checker

    REPLAY · REVIEW

    Paste this week's log (copy the text from your site, headings included). The checker uses your year's guide to find each section, how much you wrote, your links and your reasons, runs the before-you-submit checks, then lists what to fix. Nothing you paste is saved or sent anywhere.

    Context

    Why this?

    Why you are making it, why it suits your skills, time and kit, who the audience is, and what you include to make it work for them. No proposal is an instant fail.

    Research

    Three kinds

    • Technical: the software and kit, why it was best for the job, guides with screenshots of how you edited.
    • Story: linked sources for facts and figures, what you used and how, and how storytelling works.
    • Stylistic: what your piece is based on and how you recreated that style.
    Reflect

    Say why

    Evaluation is where the top grades are won. Explain your decisions with "because", say what you would change, and show how feedback moved the work on.

    For parents and carers

    Supporting your young person on Sports Media

    Sports Media is a practical, coursework-based course. There are no exams: your young person builds a portfolio of real productions across two years and is graded on it. Here is how it works and how you can help.

    What

    The qualifications

    • Year 12: UAL Level 3 Diploma in Creative Practice: Art, Design and Communication.
    • Year 13: UAL Level 3 Extended Diploma in Creative Media Production and Technology.
    • Both are awarded by UAL Awarding Body (University of the Arts London) and graded Pass, Merit or Distinction.
    • They carry UCAS points, so they count towards university applications. The Creative Media Production and Technology Diploma carries 36, 60 or 84 points for Pass, Merit or Distinction.
    How

    How it is assessed

    • Every project is assessed by teachers against the UAL grading criteria. You can read them in plain language on the Grading page.
    • Each year ends with a Final Major Project: an independent piece of sports media your young person plans, makes and evaluates.
    • The evidence lives on their own portfolio website, which is what teachers and the moderator look at.
    Weekly

    The weekly log

    Every week students write a log on their portfolio site under eight set headings: what they made, the research behind it, problems they solved, and what they would do better. It is the main evidence for their grade, so a missed week makes a real difference.

    The Weekly log page shows the headings for each year and has a checker they can paste their log into.

    Help

    How you can help

    • Ask to see their portfolio website once a week and have them talk you through the latest log.
    • Ask "why did you do it that way?" The best logs explain decisions, not just list what happened.
    • Encourage them to run the log checker before they publish each week.
    • Watch and listen to what they make. Feedback from a real audience feeds straight into their evaluation.
    • Look out for sports events, clubs and local media they could film, interview or report on.
    Time

    The working week

    Year 12 Sports Media runs for 12 hours a week as three 4-hour practical sessions, using the school's 8 radio studios, 3 sound booths and edit suite. Most work is done in those sessions; the weekly log and some research happen alongside.
